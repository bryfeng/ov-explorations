Semantic layers

A semantic layer keeps its rules, its state and its own order.

Banks, registries, payment networks, identity systems and agent platforms are semantic layers. Each defines what an event means, decides whether it is valid, and derives its state from its own events. None of that changes.

What is proposed is one addition: each layer also posts a fingerprint of each event to the shared record and reads its position back. Value, here, means records of economically consequential events. In every figure, the dashed line is what is proposed.

01 · Many layers, one order

Entries from unrelated layers interleave in one sequence.

A layer that follows the record takes only its own entries, in the record's order, and replays them under its own rules. The other layers' entries are opaque to it. All of them are opaque to the record.

02 · A rule can read across

A layer's rule may depend on another layer's earlier entry.

A registry delivers an asset only against a payment the bank recorded first. Order between the two comes from the record; the judgement stays in the registry. A proposal that fails the rule is never posted.

The record is necessary for this, not sufficient. Inclusion shows that a fingerprint was recorded at a position, not that the payment was valid; the bank's rules decided that. Proposed.

03 · Anyone can replay

State is derived from events.

Two independent readers that replay the same layer's entries under the same rules reach the same state. The record supplies the entries and their order; the rules supply the meaning. A reader that finds the final history changed beneath it stops rather than continue on a different history.

Whether shared conventions form between layers and the record, as one layer or several, is open.
